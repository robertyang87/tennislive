# Narrative contract — copy and cuts are one artefact

Material is the cheap half. What makes this column work is copy locked to the timeline: every line lands on the second whose picture proves it.

## Write against the timeline, never before it

Draft the beat list first, then find the window that shows each beat, then write the line to that window's length and content. A line written before the window exists will be defended after the window is found, and the picture loses.

The window is the harder constraint. When a line does not fit its window, cut the line; do not stretch the window into a shot that shows something else.

## Skeleton

1. **Result first.** The strongest single frame of the whole film, no narration, live sound only. Never the opening seconds of a source — official cuts run chronologically, so their first frames are the least dramatic ones.
2. **The landing point in one sentence.** What changed, or the sharpest contrast. Not coordinates, not ranking, not identity already printed on the poster.
3. **Who this is**, only as much as a viewer needs to understand the rest.
4. **The arc**, chronological, one job per beat.
5. **The turn** — the moment the story is actually about.
6. **The landing**, resolving the tension opened in 2.

## Rules that decide individual lines

- **Name the person the picture shows.** Chinese drops subjects across sentences; across a cut the viewer re-anchors to whoever is on screen. Either write the name or write a line about what is visibly happening.
- **Say the thing the picture cannot.** Never narrate what is already visible. Do narrate what is off-screen and verified.
- **Quote the subject over paraphrasing them.** Their own words carry weight a summary cannot; keep them as live sound with bilingual subtitles.
- **Do not read a scoreboard that is burned into the frame.** The viewer already read it.
- **Every absolute claim needs two independent sources**, recorded in the spec. Counting claims (“only N times, all N won”) are absolute claims wearing a different coat.
- **Prefer a closed count to an open one.** Titles won cannot shrink; finals reached can.
- **Write the limiting scope into the sentence itself.** “The only one he lost” means something different alone than it does after “of those four finals”.

## Pacing

A film of this column runs as long as the story needs; there is no upper bound and completeness beats brevity. Length is only ever a symptom — if it drags, the problem is a beat doing no work, not the minute count.

Leave room. A window carries a line plus the live sound around it; a line that fills its window edge to edge leaves the film no air.

## What disqualifies a draft

Praise with no fact under it. A recap with no turn. An arc that only makes sense if you already follow the sport. A landing that is really a schedule note about the next match. Any sentence whose picture shows someone else.
