Share: 28-year-old is first American champion in tournament history Frances Tiafoe forged American history and simultaneously hit a new personal milestone with a classy championship-match display on Sunday at the Terra Wortmann Open. The 28-year-old downed his good friend Taylor Fritz 6-4, 6-4 on the Halle grass to win the biggest title of his career and become the first American to lift the trophy in the history of the ATP 500 event (since 1993). Tiafoe, who won all three of his previous tour-level crowns at ATP 250 level, dropped just seven points on serve in the final, according to Infosys ATP Stats, to cap a memorable debut appearance at the German grass tournament. Before Sunday, Tiafoe had gone 0-4 in finals above ATP 250 level, including a defeat to Fritz in Tokyo in 2022. He also suffered recent heartbreak at Roland Garros, where he lost a five-hour, 26-minute marathon fourth-round clash with Matteo Arnaldi, yet the American has impressed so far this grass season with a quarter-final appearance in Stuttgart and now his Halle win. Tiafoe’s win against Fritz was his third against a Top-10 opponent this week at heristo-arena, where he also saw off World No. 10 Flavio Cobolli and No. 4 Felix Auger-Aliassime. “I’ve lost a lot of 500 finals. I’ve lost a lot of finals in my career,” Tiafoe said. “This is big. I just want to say one of my favourite scriptures: The pain that you’re feeling does not compare to the joy that is coming. Obviously, that has been proven very true. “A couple of weeks ago, I had one of the toughest losses of my career at the French Open, and to come back and have a good week in Stuttgart and win here for my biggest title, beating the players I did, it’s a huge testimony to that quote. It’s something I’ve been living by.” Tiafoe appeared to be on song from the start in his second ATP Tour title match of the year. As well as serving with power and precision throughout, he also made immediate inroads on return. Although he let slip a break point in the third game and three more in the fifth, the unseeded American made no mistake with his next opportunity to break for 4-3. In the second set, Tiafoe did the damage early. He notched his decisive break of Fritz’s serve in the opening game and showed admirably few nerves in serving out for just his second victory in nine Lexus ATP Head2Head meetings with Fritz. “I don’t even know what clip I was serving in the first set, but I felt like I couldn’t miss one,” Tiafoe reflected. “I returned really well. I had no troubles on my serve the whole match, and it just feels good to get this done. I played a clean match, but I had to. He’s a hell of a player and a hell of a competitor too. I knew he was going to make it hard out there at the end and I played some great tennis. Luck was on my way a little bit.” With his Halle triumph, Tiafoe also ensured he will on Monday return to the Top 20 in the PIF ATP Rankings after just five weeks away. He will rise nine spots to begin next week as the World No. 19.
