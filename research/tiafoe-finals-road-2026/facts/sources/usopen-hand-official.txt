Frances Tiafoe’s ‘out-of-body’ run continues at 2026 US Open | Official Site of the 2026 US Open Tennis Championships - A USTA Event Frances Tiafoe’s ‘out-of-body’ run continues at 2026 US Open Richard Osborn / Monday, 7 September 2026 12:05 PM EDT Frances Tiafoe sees it as a "wake-up call," one that just happened to come as his tenure in professional tennis hit the 10-year mark. Feeling stagnant, his career at a crossroads, the Marylander decided to make some sweeping changes. Hovering around the .500 mark and two years removed from his last tour-level singles title, Tiafoe shut down his 2025 campaign in October, parted ways with coach David Witt and longtime team member Jordi Arconada, hired Mark Kovacs, and refreshed his outlook. Video: Frances Tiafoe Press Conference The results were immediate. Between February and June, he would win 23 of 31 matches, including a run to the biggest title of his career on grass in Halle, his first ATP 500 crown coming at his pal/countryman Taylor Fritz ’s expense, 6-4, 6-4. Last month, he played his way into his second ATP Masters 1000 final in Cincinnati only days after undergoing surgery on his right hand. “The biggest thing on the mental side is, number one, just showing up every week,” said the 11th seed. “I’ve been doing that. It shows. With my game, it's just play to win. Play aggressive, use my whole arsenal at the right time. It's been working well.” Tiafoe a ‘different person’ at 2026 US Open Now Tiafoe is back into the quarterfinals of the US Open for the fourth time in five years thanks to a 7-6(1), 6-4, 7-6(6) takedown of Daniil Medvedev in Louis Armstrong Stadium, a venue that has all but become a second home to the 28-year-old American. With intermittent chants of ‘ONE, TWO, THREE, FOE!’, the crowd all but lifted him into the Queens sky. “It’s an out-of-body experience, no question,” said Tiafoe, now 10-0 in ‘Louie’. “There’s no real explaining that type of feeling. It's like you're not even touching the ground at one point. The energy is so high, and it seems like the level of play matches that. At the end of the [third-set] breaker, every point was just stupid. They get excited. I’m amped up. This is a fun time regardless. In tennis, there’s only one winner, but you feel like the game won it sometimes. It’s pretty cool.” Everyone knows Tiafoe is a fan of those big-stage moments. It’s what, he says, he plays for. Now he’s earned another, this time in the cavernous environs of the biggest tennis-only venue in the world: the 24,000-seat Arthur Ashe Stadium. Next up is an all-American matchup with 22-year-old compatriot Alex Michelsen . It’s yet another step on his journey. His mantra, after all, has been the Bible verse Romans 8:18: “The pain that you’ve been feeling, can’t compare to the joy that’s coming.” “It stands true every day,” said, currently two spots from his career-high No. 10 in the ATP rankings. “Every day is a different challenge. From where I was to what I'm doing now, it 100 percent stands true. You just have to keep going. Life is going to hit you. It’s not always peaches and cream every day.
