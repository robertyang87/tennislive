Bryan Armen Graham

For 23 years they have come and gone. Andy Roddick tried again. Andre Agassi made one final run. James Blake, Mardy Fish, Sam Querrey, John Isner, Tommy Paul and Taylor Fritz all carried the hopes of American men’s tennis deep into the second week of major championships, only to thrash against the ceiling of the sport’s European hegemony time and again.

Now Ben Shelton gets his chance. The fearless 23-year-old from Atlanta surged into his first career grand slam final on a feverish Friday night at the US Open, completing a 4-6, 6-3, 6-3, 7-5 fightback over Frances Tiafoe in a pulsating all-American semi-final beneath the lights of Arthur Ashe Stadium. The result leaves him one victory from becoming the first man from the United States to win one of the sport’s four bedrock tournaments since Roddick triumphed here in 2003.

Shelton is told that while Andy Roddick remains the last American man to win the title, he will become the first black man to play in the US Open final since Arthur Ashe in 1972. “It’s a lot of emotion,” Shelton says. “My dad, my mom have sacrificed a lot for me to be in this position today. My dad lost his mom earlier this year, and she’s the reason that he was able to play tennis. Without her, there’s no Ben Shelton standing here today. So this one is for her. This one is for her.”

Shelton then turns his attention to Sunday and the Arthur Ashe Stadium crowd.

“I’m going for it here,” he says. “I’m gonna leave it all on the court. I hope you guys are coming back with me. Let’s do this for the USA.”

Before leaving the court, Shelton also acknowledges the significance of the date.

“It’s a difficult day to be out here playing,” he says. “My thoughts and prayers go out to all the families who were impacted by 9/11. This is one of the greatest, greatest cities in the world, and one of the biggest tragedies. Thoughts and prayers to everyone involved and affected.”

“[Frances] like a big brother to me, to be honest,” says Shelton in his on-court interview. “Every time that we get to take this court together, Arthur Ashe, and play each other is super special. But this is where we want to be, at the tail end of this tournament. This is both our favorite tournament in the world, our favorite court in the world, and to be able to light it up in front of you guys is the best feeling that there is.”

He’s asked what is different about the Ben Shelton of today compared to the one who reached his first US Open semi-final three years ago.

“I think I’ve grown up,” he says. “I’m more mature, more focused. I know what my goal is. I know what my job is out here,and I’m 100% committed to those goals.”

He continues: “It’s not easy playing here on Arthur Ashe. You’re playing in front of all your favorite athletes and movie stars, so to be able to keep your head down and focus for over three hours is not an easy task. I keep on looking up at the screen like, ‘Oh, shoot, Jalen Brunson’s here.’”

Preamble

Hello and welcome to Arthur Ashe Stadium for tonight’s all-American US Open men’s semi-final between Frances Tiafoe and Ben Shelton. For the third time in four years, the two friends and crowd favorites will meet at Flushing Meadows. Shelton won their first New York encounter, overpowering Tiafoe in four sets in the 2023 quarter-finals on his way to a breakthrough semi-final appearance at 20. Tiafoe answered 12 months later, coming from two sets to one down to win a five-set thriller in the third round. Shelton leads their overall series 3-1, including a straight-sets victory in Washington last summer.

Tonight’s rubber match is considerably bigger. The winner will become the first black American man to reach the US Open singles final since Arthur Ashe in 1972 and the first to reach any grand slam singles final since MaliVai Washington at Wimbledon in 1996. He will also become only the fifth American man this century to reach the final in New York, after Pete Sampras, Andre Agassi, Andy Roddick and Taylor Fritz.

Both men arrive after extraordinary five-set quarter-finals. Tiafoe somehow escaped Alex Michelsen after trailing by two sets, falling a break behind in the third and then going 3-0 down in the fifth. His 5-7, 3-6, 7-5, 6-3, 7-6 (6) victory put him into his third US Open semi-final in five years, matching Agassi for the most by an American man this century.

Shelton’s route was even more spectacular. The 23-year-old toppled defending champion Carlos Alcaraz 6-7 (5), 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 7-6 (7) in a late-night classic that finished at 3.33am, ending the Spaniard’s 18-match winning streak at grand slam level and securing his first victory over a top-three opponent after starting his career 0-17 against them.

One American dream ends tonight and another moves into Sunday’s final against Alexander Zverev with a shot of ending the country’s 23-year wait for a men’s major champion.

The first ball will not be at 7pm local time as scheduled while Ashe changes over for the separately ticketed night session. We’ll update you with an approximate start time as we hear.
