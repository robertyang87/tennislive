Shelton beats Tiafoe to reach first Grand Slam final at 2026 US Open | Official Site of the 2026 US Open Tennis Championships - A USTA Event Shelton beats Tiafoe to reach first Grand Slam final at 2026 US Open Adesina O. Koiki / Friday, 11 September 2026 10:52 PM EDT WHAT HAPPENED Ben Shelton is into a Grand Slam final for the first time after defeating fellow American Frances Tiafoe , 4-6, 6-3, 6-3, 7-5, in the 2026 US Open semifinals on Friday night in Arthur Ashe Stadium. The top-ranked American man will play top-seeded Alexander Zverev for the title on Sunday. The No. 8 seed also became the first Black American man to reach a US Open singles final since Arthur Ashe in 1972. Shelton is dialed in, alright, and we’re talking about much more than just his signature on-court celebration. Even after his prior match ended well after 12:00 a.m., the American's dream of winning a Grand Slam title hasn’t struck midnight just yet, and on Sunday, he will attempt to finally bring a men's major singles title back to the United States. The 23-year-old Shelton, after knocking out defending champion Carlos Alcaraz in the early hours of Wednesday morning, was none the worse for wear on Friday night as he came back from a set down in defeating No. 11 seed Tiafoe in four sets. “He’s like a big brother to me to be honest,” Shelton said of Tiafoe during the on-court interview. “When we get together on this court in Arthur Ashe, it’s super special. It’s our favorite tournament in the world.” Shelton will attempt to become the first American man to win a Grand Slam title since Andy Roddick at the 2003 US Open on Sunday when he takes on Zverev, who defeated Karen Khachanov in the first semifinal of the day. Video: Tiafoe vs. Shelton | 2026 Semifinal Inside the stadium bearing the name of the last Black American man to play in (1972) and win (1968) the US Open men’s singles final, Arthur Ashe, both Shelton and Tiafoe took the court knowing history was made upon their entrance—and more so when the first ball was put in play. Upon commencement, both put on a dazzling display of baseline power and net finesse befitting of the occasion, enrapturing the Arthur Ashe Stadium crowd as the two split the first couple of sets. Shelton is coached by his father, former tour pro Bryan Shelton, who made history in 1991 when he became the first Black American man to win an ATP Tour title after Ashe did so in 1978. Ben now has the chance to join Ashe as a Grand Slam champion as both father and son continue to carry the trailblazing legacy left by the tennis champ and civil rights icon. “It’s a lot of emotion. My dad, my mom sacrificed a lot for me to be in this position today,” Shelton said. “My dad lost his mom earlier this year, and she’s the reason that he was able to play tennis. Without her, there’s no Ben Shelton standing here today. This one is for her.” Family support helps Shelton win  'epic' vs. Alcaraz Shelton turned up his game, particularly his serve and backhand, in the third set. He smacked 12 winners in the frame as his first serve percentage was an astounding 82%. Shelton broke in the fifth game of the set with back-to-back backhand passes to close out the game, and then broke again in an epic 15-minute game that featured seven deuce points to clinch the set. He kept the pressure on in the fourth set and earned the decisive break in the final game as Tiafoe attempted to force a tiebreak. In the match, Shelton’s serve proved too much for even an excellent returner like Tiafoe, as the former put in 75% of his first serves, cracked 20 aces and hit almost twice as many winners as Frances (47-27). WHAT IT MEANS Shelton extended his head-to-head advantage over Tiafoe to 4-1 and, more importantly, won the New York rubber match between the two after splitting the first two US Open matchups in 2023 (Shelton) and 2024 (Tiafoe). “I think I’ve grown up. I’m more mature, more focused,” Shelton said when comparing the player he is now to the one in 2023 when he made his other US Open semifinal. “I know what my goal is. I know what my job is out here, and I’m 100% committed to those goals. It’s not easy playing here on Arthur Ashe.” Part of what made it hard was all of the celebrities who came out for the Friday night showdown, including one who’s enjoying his current tour as the unofficial king of New York. “You’re playing in front of all your favorite athletes and movie stars, so to be able to keep your head down and focus for over three hours is not an easy task. I keep on looking up at the screen like, ‘Oh, shoot, Jalen Brunson’s here.’” Standing in the way of Shelton’s maiden Grand Slam title is the top-seeded Zverev, who has defeated the former Florida Gator in each of their five all-time meetings. Zverev books second trip to US Open final MATCH POINT On Sunday, Shelton has a chance to tie Tiafoe for the most wins by an American man at Grand Slams this year at 12. Of course, if he does tie Frances, Shelton also will do so as he holds the US Open championship trophy aloft. “I’m gonna need you guys on Sunday to bring the energy,” Shelton implored to the crowd about Sunday’s tilt. “We’re going for it here. I’m gonna leave it all on the court. I hope you guys are coming back. Do this for the USA.” The match also capped an emotional day in New York and the country as today marked the 25th anniversary of 9/11. “It’s a difficult day to be out here playing,” said Shelton. “My thoughts and prayers go out to all the families who were impacted by 9/11.
