Shelton ends Alcaraz's US Open title defense in late-night five-setter

Jay Paris / 
Wednesday, 9 September 2026 03:35 AM EDT

WHAT HAPPENED

Top-ranked American Ben Shelton upset defending champion Carlos Alcaraz, 6-7(5), 6-1, 6-3, 1-6, 7-6(7), in the quarterfinals of the 2026 US Open on Tuesday night inside Arthur Ashe Stadium.

No. 8 seed Shelton, with his first triumph over a player ranked in the Top 3, advances to face another American, Frances Tiafoe, in Friday’s semifinals.

Alcaraz, the Spanish No. 3 seed, won a first-set tiebreak and showed little sign that things would soon go askew. But they did, as a poised Shelton won nine straight games at one point to impose his will on Alcaraz and dominate the two-time US Open champion in a manner few expected.

It was the latest finish in US Open history, with Shelton firing the clinching 146-mph ace at 3:33 a.m.

This verdict wasn’t a fluke as the left-handed Shelton’s massive serving, dynamite returns and angled groundstrokes were too much for Alcaraz. Shelton snapped the Spaniard's 18-match winning streak in majors with the biggest triumph of his career in a four-hour, 28-minute match.

Up until Tuesday, Alcaraz looked in fine form in his return from a four-and-a-half month layoff because of a right wrist injury. But he hadn’t faced someone like Shelton, who matched his swagger and eclipsed his firepower.

“I mean, it’s incredible,’’ Shelton said after smacking 34 winners with 35 unforced errors. “I always say this is the greatest sports city in the world, the greatest stadium in the world.”

It’ll be debated if this was also the greatest match. If not, it’s near the top of a short list.

“Are you not entertained,’’ Bryan Shelton, Ben’s dad and coach, said at one point.

There were so many sublime points in the match that it’s almost a disservice to bring them up, because one could recite them all. The shotmaking was electric, the court coverage was amazing and the grit these two competitors showed was off the charts.

All with the clock ticking and the calendar flipping days.

Today, yesterday and tomorrow—pick the day, because this match will be the topic du jour, among sports fans in general and tennis boosters in particular, for years to come.

As promising as Shelton’s summer has been, with him winning in Montreal in the US Open lead-up, few expected him to knock off Alcaraz. The Spaniard had won the last two and three of the last four majors he played and was on a 31-1 streak at the Grand Slams since Roland Garros 2025. 

Shelton? He was 0-17 against Top 3 players and 1-6 versus Top 10 rivals at the majors.

Instead it’s Shelton headed for his third Grand Slam semifinal, and his second in Queens.

Despite the late hour, or early hour, depending on your perspective, the Ashe crowd was raucous to the very end.

“For me it’s always about the people in the crowd,’’ said Shelton, after handing Alcaraz his second loss in 17 five-set matches at Flushing Meadows. “Whether it’s my coaches, my team over there, my friends and family over here, or the city of New York tonight that showed up until 3 a.m. with 'USA' chants until the very end, that was what kept me going and that was what got me the win tonight.’’

Tonight’s the night for Shelton as he became the first American to eliminate Alcaraz at a major on the 14th attempt.

“I’m just so grateful and so blessed,’’ Shelton said. “God blessed me in so many ways to be here in front of you guys tonight. It is an absolute honor and sharing the court with Carlos is an absolute honor.”

The first set, after each player secured a late break, went to a tiebreak that produced some of the tournament’s most entertaining tennis. Both players felt the tension with a handful of mini-breaks and it included a point that had the crowd on its feet, and briefly, Alcaraz on his back.

Shelton was two points from the set at 5-3, when Alcaraz came roaring back to win four straight points to seize the tiebreak.

The second set had spectators rubbing their eyes and it wasn’t because it was past the midnight hour. In a rare lapse by Alcaraz, he lost his focus and accuracy, with Shelton breaking him three times as he rolled to a 6-1 shocker. Alcaraz became frustrated and befuddled, as his dominance stunningly slipped away.

The third set was nearly as one-sided as the fire that usually burns in Alcaraz’s soul was extinguished by the shotmaking of Shelton. He sprinted to a 3-0 cushion and cruised in from there for two-sets-to-one lead, helped by Alcaraz missing shots that he usually puts away easily.

Alcaraz found some juice in the fourth set and displayed the championship pedigree of a player with seven Grand Slam titles. He broke Shelton in the frame’s second game and continued to lift his performance, just before it was too late.

Then came the fifth set which will long be remembered as it produced the third 10-point tiebreaker of a dramatic day in Ashe—but not before Shelton came within five points of the win when holding break points at 4-4.

In the breaker, Shelton raced to a 3-1 lead. Then Alcaraz roared back for a 5-3 edge. It was 6-6, then 8-6 for Shelton, when he got a mini-break.

Two points later, thanks to just his seventh ace, Shelton slayed one of the sport’s biggest stars.

“I cherish every moment that I am out here,’’ said an exhausted Shelton, over the moon and into the semifinals.

After the first set, it looked as if he would be out of the tournament. After five sets, it looked as if he was the king of the tennis world.

WHAT IT MEANS

Shelton owns a 3-1 lead over Tiafoe, although they have split their two pairings at the US Open, squaring off in 2023 and 2024. All the meetings have been on hard courts, save Shelton beating him in the Houston final, on clay, in 2024.

Tiafoe, seeded No. 11, has advanced to three US Open semifinals, all coming in the last five years. He leads all players in hard-court wins on ATP Tour this season with 26.

Recovery will be crucial for Shelton, considering the late hour he finished and the physical demands it takes to beat Alcaraz in five sets. But with both Americans trying to snap Uncle Sam’s 23-year drought without a major singles title, and both coming off gruelling five-setters, you won't find any excuses on Friday night.

"Two days [off], yeah, I think I'll be all right," Shelton said.

MATCH POINT

Shelton is 34-8 on North American soil since July last year, highlighted by winning ATP Masters 1000 titles in Toronto last year and Montreal last month without dropping a set.
