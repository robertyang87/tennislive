Alcaraz dispatches Zverev, wins first Nitto ATP Finals crown
22 November 2026
By Sam Jacot
© Corinne Dubreuil/ATP Tour
Share:
Spaniard earns 6-2, 7-6(6) victory in Turin
Carlos Alcaraz captured his first Nitto ATP Finals title on Sunday in Turin, where he overcame World No. 1 Alexander Zverev 6-2, 7-6(7) to become the third Spaniard to triumph at the prestigious year-end event.
Alcaraz fell in the final to Jannik Sinner last year but ensured he would not suffer more heartbreak in Turin to end the year on a high. Despite playing with heavy strapping on his left leg, Alcaraz moved freely and was sharp on return to become the first player this week to break Zverev’s serve and maintain his flawless record in finals against the German (3-0).
The 23-year-old showcased his trademark variety, using his backhand slice to disrupt Zverev’s rhythm, while displaying the big-match mentality that has become a hallmark of his career. Just as he had during his dramatic five-set victory against the German at the Australian Open, Alcaraz found a way through the biggest moments throughout to secure his sixth title of the season and 30th overall.
After winning the opening set, Alcaraz saved three consecutive set points in the second-set tie-break to triumph. The 23-year-old rallied from 3/6, reeling off six of the next seven points to seal victory after a tense finale. Alcaraz collapsed to the ground in celebration following his two-hour, one-minute win and then, sporting a beaming smile, embraced his team courtside.
"For me it's been an amazing week," Alcaraz said. "I couldn't have ended the week in a better way. I'm just really proud and really happy with everything I have done this week. I've been through really difficult moments within myself, with my team, and we went through everything playing great tennis. This trophy is something I have been chasing for a really long time. It means the world to me."
Alcaraz is the third Spaniard to win the Nitto ATP Finals, alongside Manuel Orantes, who triumphed in 1976, and Alex Corretja, who lifted the trophy in 1998. He is also just the fourth man to win all four major titles and the season-ending championships, joining Andre Agassi, Roger Federer and Novak Djokovic. In February, Alcaraz became the youngest player to complete the Career Grand Slam when he won the Australian Open.
"Today's final, I needed to put the three c's that my grandpa always tells me that I have to give every time that I step on the court for every match and every practice," Alcaraz said. "He's watching at home, so I just want to say I love you to my grandparents. In Spanish it's 'Cabeza, corazon, cajones', in English it is 'Head, heart, balls', so I think today that was about it."
The triumph is another remarkable milestone in Alcaraz's season, which was disrupted due to injury. Alcaraz missed more than four months after suffering a wrist injury at the ATP 500 event in Barcelona in April, returning at the US Open in August, where he reached the quarter-finals. Alcaraz has since been in red-hot form, winning titles in Tokyo, Vienna, Paris and Turin, compiling a 22-1 record during that stretch, according to the Infosys ATP Win/Loss Index.
Alcaraz’s fitness had been a talking point throughout the week after he received a medical timeout for a left leg issue during Wednesday’s match against Daniil Medvedev. The Spaniard went on to complete a perfect 3-0 round-robin campaign, defeating Flavio Cobolli on Friday. The second seed then battled past Ben Shelton in a deciding-set tie-break to reach the final, showing no obvious signs of discomfort to move into the championship match.

.
In Sunday’s title showdown, Zverev survived a nine-minute opening game in which he fended off three break points, but Alcaraz soon found a way through. Striking the ball cleanly from the baseline, the Spaniard became the first player to break Zverev’s serve all week in the third game, converting to love to seize early control.
Alcaraz was then put under pressure on his own serve, but responded impressively to preserve his advantage. In a 10-minute fourth game, the No. 3 player in the PIF ATP Rankings saved three break points, twice with powerful crosscourt backhands. Alcaraz built on that escape to move further ahead, breaking Zverev twice more in the first set to lead.
Alcaraz continued where he left off at the start of the second set, once again breaking Zverev's serve to love. Even when Zverev was on the front foot in the rallies, Alcaraz  demonstrated great agility to scamper from side to side and successfully hang in points. The 23-year-old then recovered from squandering a 4-2 advantage, keeping his composure from 4-5, 0/30 to fend off the danger, with Zverev playing more freely to hit through the court.
The Spaniard found first serves and big forehands to regain control, and then rallied from 3/6 in the second-set tie-break. He saved three set points and then sealed victory on his second match point when he struck a backhand winner. Alcaraz, who used the drop shot consistently in the closing stages, now leads Zverev 8-6 in their Lexus ATP Head2Head series and it is the first time the Spaniard has defeated the German indoors (1-3). Alcaraz has also won his past nine matches against Top-10 opponents.
Alcaraz departs Turin with a record $5,171,000 champion’s payout after going 5-0 at the tournament. The second seed defeated Frances Tiafoe in his opening match before adding victories against Medvedev, Cobolli, Shelton and Zverev to complete his title run.
By triumphing in northern Italy, Alcaraz has also significantly closed the gap to World No. 2 Sinner in the PIF ATP Rankings. The Spaniard began the week 1,850 points behind the Italian but is now within 350 points, with 2025 champion Sinner having missed the event due to injury.
Zverev, meanwhile, remains at No. 1 with 10,350 points. The German was bidding to win the Nitto ATP Finals for the third time, having previously triumphed in 2018 and 2021. The 29-year-old, who did not drop a set en route to the final, has enjoyed a career-best season. The German won his first major titles at Roland Garros and the US Open before he climbed to No. 1 for the first time in November.
Did You Know?
Alcaraz is the third player to win both the Nitto ATP Finals and the Next Gen ATP Finals, joining Stefanos Tsitsipas and Sinner.
