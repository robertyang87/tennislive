Alcaraz survives Shelton to reach final of Nitto ATP Finals
21 November 2026
By Sam Jacot
© Corinne Dubreuil/ATP Tour
Share:
Spaniard faces Zverev or Medvedev in final on Sunday
Carlos Alcaraz demonstrated his fighting qualities to return to the Nitto ATP Finals championship match on Saturday in Turin, where he battled from a set down to sink Ben Shelton 3-6, 6-3, 7-6(1).
In front of a capacity crowd inside Inalpi Arena, Alcaraz improved throughout a thrilling two-hour, one-minute clash to fire past the American. The World No. 3, who played with heavy strapping on his upper left leg, timed his groundstokes cleanly from the second set onwards and and showcased his big-match mentality in the crucial moments down the home straight to extend his winning streak to 13 matches and remain unbeaten on indoor courts this year (14-0).
“I was a little bit frustrated at the beginning because I didn't start the match the way I wanted to, to be honest,” Alcaraz said in his on-court interview. “And again, someone like Ben, big serve, big playing from the baseline, I think he just pushes you to the limit very time. I didn't start that well. And there was that break early. It was tough for me to deal with.
“But just happy that I came back in the second set playing such great tennis, step by step, focused on all my things and ended up playing good tennis. The third set was even closer. I think he raised the level a little bit from the second set. Myself as well. Great, great points, great games and just happy to play a great tennis in the tie-break. I think this win matters. So, excited for tomorrow, and happy with everything today.”
Alcaraz lost to Jannik Sinner in the title match in Turin last season but will aim to go one step further and lift the prestigious year-end trophy for the first time when he faces Alexander Zverev or Daniil Medvedev in the championship match on Sunday at 6 p.m. CET.
The 23-year-old joined Rafael Nadal as just the second Spaniard in history to reach multiple finals at the event. Alcaraz, who went 3-0 in round-robin play, received a medical timeout during his match against Medvedev on Wednesday due to an issue with his left leg. The 29-time tour-level titlist returned to court on Friday against Flavio Cobolli and moved freely, and he did not show signs of discomfort against Shelton, who was competing in the semi-finals in Turin for the first time.
Shelton made a rapid start inside Inalpi Arena, breaking Alcaraz immediately when he fired a forehand winner off an Alcaraz first serve. The American quickly moved into a 4-1 lead and then fended off two break points with big serving at 4-2. Alcaraz’s movement was seemingly hampered during periods of the opening set, while his timing was slightly off, with Shelton remaining aggressive and solid to lead.
The key moment in the second set came in the eighth game, when Alcaraz broke the Shelton delivery for the first time. The 23-year-old delivered a one-two punch off the backhand wing to open up the court and conjure up two break points. He then took his first chance when Shelton struck wide. Alcaraz, in full flow, rolled through his next service game, hitting a backhand winner crosscourt to force a decider.
With little to separate, a tie-break decided the clash. As he has done throughout his career, Alcaraz increased his intensity and found his best level to earn victory. The Spaniard let out a roar after triumphing and then embraced Shelton in a warm exchange at the net.
Alcaraz won 80 per cent of points behind his first serve, his second-best mark of the week, while landing 73 per cent of his first deliveries. The third seed also struck 33 winners against 21 unforced errors, compared to Shelton’s even 30-30 count.
Shelton showed impressive athleticism at times to dispatch smashes and stretch for volleys, but was unable to produce his best quality in the third-set tie-break. The No. 4 player in the PIF ATP Rankings, who has won four titles in 2026, ends his career-best season holding a 47-21 record, according to the Infosys ATP Win/Loss Index.
Alcaraz missed more than four months of the season due to a wrist injury he suffered at the ATP 500 event in Barcelona in April, returning at the US Open in August, when he lost to Shelton in a fifth-set tie-break in the quarter-finals. The Spaniard has since lifted trophies in Tokyo, Vienna and Paris, downing Shelton en route to the title in the French capital. Alcaraz now leads Shelton 5-1 in the pair’s Lexus ATP Head2Head series.
