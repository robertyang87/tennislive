Alcaraz on Nitto ATP Finals title: 'This one is special'
22 November 2026
By Sam Jacot
© Corinne Dubreuil/ATP Tour
Share:
Spaniard reflects on winning the prestigious year-end event
Last year, Carlos Alcaraz fell just short at the Nitto ATP Finals, losing to Jannik Sinner in the championship match. On Sunday in Turin, the 23-year-old defeated Alexander Zverev to leave Italy with the trophy, triumphing at the prestigious year-end event for the first time.
After defeating Zverev in the final, the Spaniard reflected on his dream week..
“This one is special,” Alcaraz said in his post-match press conference. “This one means the world to me, to my team, to my family. They know how hard I've been chasing this moment, this trophy, this tournament, how hard I've been working for this tournament, to lift this trophy. So finally, to be able to lift the trophy here, it's something amazing, the feeling that I have right now.
“It's difficult to put into words, to be honest. So I'm really proud of everything I've done this week, with my team, with my close people, that without them, it wouldn't be possible to lift this trophy.”
In an impressive performance on Sunday, Alcaraz saved three set points in the second-set tie-break, winning six of the next seven points from 3/6 to move past Zverev and get his hands on the trophy.
The 23-year-old then collapsed to the ground in celebration, before he enjoyed an emotional embrace with his team.
“It is a tournament that I really wanted to win one year. I was chasing that moment really bad since a really long time. Once I got it, a lot of emotions came to me, came to my team. I was looking at them. They were screaming, they were celebrating, they were everything. My mum was crying. She's an emotional person,” Alcaraz said.
“Seeing them there and celebrating, giving them hugs and everything was amazing. It was a really difficult two weeks for me, dealing with the pressure, dealing with the physical problems, having team meetings, making really difficult decisions, controlling myself on the court, off the court.”
Alcaraz managed a left leg issue throughout the week in Turin, but his body held up, allowing him to become just the third Spaniard to win the event, joining Manuel Orantes, who triumphed in 1976, and Alex Corretja, who lifted the trophy in 1998.
The No. 3 player in the PIF ATP Rankings is now a 30-time tour-level titlist, having won six trophies in 2026. Alcaraz also maintained his perfect record against Zverev in finals (3-0), while he improved to 8-6 in the pair’s Lexus ATP Head2Head series overall.
Alcaraz praised coach Samuel Lopez when reflecting on his season overall, which included a four-month period on the sidelines due to a wrist injury.
"He did a great job," Alcaraz said on Lopez. "I'm really happy, and I'm really proud about the job and the work Samuel did the whole year, alongside my brother Alvaro. They both did a great job, which I'm really proud of. We started the season really strong, winning Australia, winning Doha. Just coming with a lot of confidence, making the final in Monte-Carlo, great results.
"Then the injury during that time, they made it really easy. At some point I realised how good I have to feel off the court in my life to go and hit the practice court with anger, with motivation, and I think they made a really good job off the court, as well, coming from the injury, just always saying positive things.
"I would say they said really carefully everything to me that was really important and helped me a lot during the matches, during the practices, and when we're not on the tennis court. So as I said, really happy to have them in my team, having them as head coach and second coach, and it's just started. I can say that."
