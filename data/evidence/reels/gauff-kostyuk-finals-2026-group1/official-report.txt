 INDIAN WELLS, Calif. -- Coco Gauff knew coming into Thursday that even a win over Marta Kostyuk might not be enough to keep her in the FAGE WTA Finals. What she could do was give herself a reason to watch the second match. And for a while in the second set, even that looked like it would take some doing. 
 Gauff beat Kostyuk 6-4, 7-5, winning the last six games to finish 2-1 in round-robin play. But her bid for a semifinal spot ended later Thursday when Jessica Pegula beat Linda Noskova 7-6(2), 4-6, 7-5. Noskova won Group 1, and Pegula took the second semifinal spot on percentage of sets won.

 After taking the opening set, Gauff fell behind 3-0 in the second and took a medical timeout. She was later seen working with a trainer on her lower back, which she said felt tight and bothered her most on serve.  
  It didn’t immediately get better from there. Kostyuk stretched her lead to 5-1, and when Gauff served to stay in the set, she opened the game with one of her nine double faults. 
 Gauff held anyway, and once she started going after Kostyuk’s forehand, the set turned quickly. She broke for 5-3, then won eight straight points to get back to 5-5. Gauff held for 6-5, and by then it was Kostyuk who had to serve to stay in the set. Gauff broke once more to close out the match.  


  “I’m not gonna lie,” Gauff said in her postmatch press conference. “I did enter like a bit of a flow state in that second set. I honestly was just preparing to play a third at that point. But one game turned into another, and I think she got a little bit frustrated too. 
 “So that kind of helped me. And then once it was like 5-4, I felt, OK, I just need to get to [5-all]. And then once [it was 5-all], I felt like momentum was on my side, and I just needed to continue to keep my foot on the gas.” 
 Gauff said she first noticed the tightness in her previous match. 
 “It started a little bit in my last match, but it’s not like pain,” she said. “It’s just blocked a little bit. So moving and stuff, I was fine. It was just a little bit on the serve. I was struggling sometimes on the forehand, but it’s OK. I think it’s one of those things that with some time will go away. Definitely can play through it and stuff. I just have to keep it loose.” 
  How the group was decided  
 Gauff, Pegula and Noskova all finished 2-1. Noskova went 5-2 in sets to win the group, followed by Pegula at 5-3 and Gauff at 4-3. 
 That’s where the second set of Gauff’s opening win over Pegula came back into play. Gauff had a set point that would have ended the match in straight sets. Pegula forced a third, and the set she won that day ultimately gave her the edge over Gauff. 
 Before Pegula and Noskova took the court, Gauff said she had a backup plan for Saturday in Los Angeles, where Tyler, the Creator’s Camp Flog Gnaw Carnival is taking place this weekend. 
 “But I’m hoping not to be there,” Gauff said.
