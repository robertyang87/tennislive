WTA Finals: Svitolina bounces back, Muchova now needs Andreeva to stumble

Beaten by Mirra Andreeva in her opening match, the Ukrainian responded by defeating Karolina Muchova in three sets. The Czech, meanwhile, no longer controls her own fate and will need a Sabalenka loss to stay in the running.

Jules Hypolite
11/11/2026, 22:22
2 min to read
Follow us
Indian Wells Muchová Svitolina WTA Finals

Another match marked by errors and a lack of rhythm at the WTA Finals.

To open the fourth day of competition, Elina Svitolina and Karolina Muchova were meeting for the fifth time on tour.

The Ukrainian led their head-to-head 3-1, although two of her three wins dated back to 2019.

Svitolina bounces back after her loss to Andreeva

Both players came into this match needing to respond after losing their opening matches in Group 2. Muchova had fallen just short against Aryna Sabalenka (7-6, 7-5), while Svitolina had been soundly outplayed by Mirra Andreeva (6-4, 6-0).

In this match already decisive for qualification, it was ultimately the Ukrainian who found the resources to win in three sets (3-6, 6-2, 6-4). A victory that keeps her hopes of reaching the semifinals alive.

A first win at the WTA Finals since 2019

Both players struggled for long stretches to find their rhythm on the Indian Wells court. In total, they committed 68 unforced errors and 14 double faults.

Muchova first took advantage of her opponent's many inaccuracies to take control. After dropping the first set, Svitolina even had to call the trainer to have her right leg examined.

But the Ukrainian quickly regained control of the match. More solid on her serve, she notably won 88% of points behind her second serve in the second set to level the contest.

Muchova will need Andreeva's help

In a tense third set, the world No. 6 ultimately made the difference. She improves her three-set record this season, now standing at 14 wins and just 3 losses in that category.

After two hours of play, Svitolina thus claims her first win at the WTA Finals since 2019, the year of her last appearance at the tournament, and can still hope to qualify for the final four.

Muchova, despite two losses in Group 2, is not yet eliminated.

A straight-sets defeat for Mirra Andreeva against Aryna Sabalenka would keep her alive in the tournament.
