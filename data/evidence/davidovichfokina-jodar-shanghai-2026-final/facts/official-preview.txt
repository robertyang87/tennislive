Share Facebook Twitter Linked In Email A breakthrough star and a resurgent Spaniard will clash in the Rolex Shanghai Masters final Sunday, when Rafael Jodar and Alejandro Davidovich Fokina meet in the championship match at the Chinese ATP Masters 1000 event. Jodar has enjoyed a breakthrough debut ATP Tour season, while 27-year-old Davidovich Fokina has claimed his first two titles this year. After impressive runs through the Shanghai draw, both have the chance to claim the biggest title of their career Sunday at 4:30 p.m. local time (China Standard Time) at the Qi Zhong Tennis Center. For Jodar, reaching his first Masters 1000 final marks another milestone in his first season on the ATP Tour. The 20-year-old has won the ATP 250 title in Marrakech and finished runner-up at the ATP 500 event in Washington. Jodar has also reached the quarter-finals at the Masters 1000 events in Madrid and Rome and the semi-finals in Montreal. Jodar arrived in Shanghai following opening-round defeats at the US Open and in Tokyo, but has responded with a series of determined performances. The Spaniard battled through four three-set matches, defeating Thiago Agustin Tirante, Jiri Lehecka and Botic van de Zandschulp before overcoming Casper Ruud in the quarter-finals. On Saturday, he secured one of the biggest wins of his career, beating former World No. 1 Daniil Medvedev to reach his maiden Masters 1000 final. Jodar is now one victory away from adding a Masters 1000 crown to his achievements. "I'm happy how the season and how the tournament is going for me," Jodar said following his semi-final win. "I think I'm having the right mentality, and I am playing well here in Shanghai. All the matches that I played in the past rounds have helped me to have the shape that I have right now, and that's very important. There's still one more match. I'll try to have the same mentality as I had in these past matches." Standing in his way is Davidovich Fokina, who is making a resurgence. The 27-year-old claimed his first ATP Tour title in Mallorca earlier this season, finally converting in his sixth tour-level final, before adding his second trophy earlier this month in Chengdu. After missing the North American hard-court swing because of a back injury, he has returned to form in Shanghai and put himself within reach of the biggest title of his career. Davidovich Fokina has impressed throughout the tournament, saving a match point to upset seventh seed Frances Tiafoe in the third round before defeating former champion Hubert Hurkacz in the fourth. He then produced one of the tournament’s biggest upsets on Saturday, when he stunned top seed Alexander Zverev to reach his second Masters 1000 final and his first since Monte-Carlo in 2022. "It doesn't matter the result, I am just thinking about every ball," Davidovich Fokina said following his semi-final triumph. "I knew that if I stayed there all match, I could get at least one chance and I took it there in the second set. Then in the third set, I think I was better physically. The early break in the third set gave me a lot of power. I focused on just playing, not [thinking] about the result, but enjoying." Experience could prove a key factor in Sunday's final. Davidovich Fokina has contested seven previous ATP Tour finals, while Jodar's performances in 2026 and his resilience in Shanghai have shown he can thrive on the biggest stages. Both have relied on hard-fought, often three-set victories, setting up an intriguing clash between a rising talent and a player enjoying the best stretch of his career. The stakes extend beyond the trophy. With a victory, Jodar would break into the Top 10 of the PIF ATP Rankings for the first time and rise to the sixth spot in the PIF ATP Live Race To Turin. The rising star is trying to qualify for the Nitto ATP Finals for the first time. Davidovich Fokina, meanwhile, has climbed 11 places to No. 15 in the PIF ATP Live Rankings this fortnight and is already assured of returning to the Top 20 on Monday. The Shanghai crown could take him past his career-high ranking of No. 14. Sunday's final therefore presents two different opportunities for a career-defining moment. But only one Spaniard will leave Shanghai with a maiden Masters 1000 crown.
