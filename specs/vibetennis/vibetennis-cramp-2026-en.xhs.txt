Cramp? Bananas and salt water? In one study, a sip of pickle juice was faster

Shanghai 2025: Sinner retires with cramps. Everyone says heat, water, salt.

In a 10-man study (cramps triggered electrically), water ended a cramp in about 134 s; pickle juice in about 85 s, while it was still mostly in the stomach and blood salt hadn't changed.

The leading explanation is in the nerves: spindles are the accelerator, tendon organs the brake. Fatigue makes the accelerator touchier and the brake weaker, so the motor nerve keeps firing. Stretching pulls the tendon and brings the brake back.

Among 210 Ironman triathletes, crampers showed no difference in electrolytes or body-mass change; they raced faster and had cramped before.

But salt matters for heavy, salty sweaters: a 17-year-old tennis player stopped cramping after adding salt. Alcaraz, Roland Garros 2023: "the main thing, it was the tension."
