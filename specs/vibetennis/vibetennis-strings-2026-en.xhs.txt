Did the strings change tennis?

1874: sheep gut. Then nylon. 1997: Kuerten wins Roland-Garros on polyester; by 2010 every man at Wimbledon used it.

Three old laws explain what strings do. Newton: in the lab, changing string material barely changes ball speed. Coulomb: the ball slides, then rolls — the 1977 "spaghetti" racket let strings slide and snap back for about 2x the spin, and was banned in 1978. Stress relaxation: tension drops about 30% right after stringing, fastest for polyester — so pros take a freshly strung racket at every ball change.

Our count from Match Charting Project men's data (volunteer-charted, aces excluded): serve-and-volley was about 40% of points in the 1990s, under 10% after 2005; at Wimbledon, 73.1% fell to 6.2% since 2020 — the years polyester spread. But rackets, balls and courts changed too; no study has isolated the strings' effect.
