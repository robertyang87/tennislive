Why does a tiebreak go 1-2-2-2? The answer is a surprise

2019 Wimbledon final: Federer won 14 more points and lost. Djokovic took all three tiebreaks.

Servers have the edge, so why not one serve each? For two equal players (65% on serve), one each and 1-2-2-2 both give the first server exactly 50%.

The real fix is "win by two": it always evens out the serves. History tested it: the 1970 US Open tiebreak was first to 5, served 2-2-2-3, so the second server got 5 serves to 4 — first server: 45.7%. In 1975 it became today's win-by-two tiebreak.

So why 1-2-2-2? Fewer switches, and nobody is always chasing. It matches the Thue–Morse sequence for four symbols.

Football's first-kicker edge shrinks as samples grow: 60% in 129 shootouts, about 55% in 1,623, none in ~7,000. In 2,621 pro tiebreaks, serving first made no significant difference.
