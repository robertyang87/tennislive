Why does a tiebreak go 1-2-2-2? The answer is a surprise

2019 Wimbledon final: Federer won 14 more points and lost. Djokovic took all three tiebreaks.

Servers have the edge, so why not one serve each? Intuition: with one each, the first server is always one serve ahead after odd points. But for two equal players (65% on serve), one each and 1-2-2-2 both give the first server exactly 50%.

The real fix is "win by two": it always evens out the serves. Play first to 7 instead and the first server gets 53.6% under either order.

So why 1-2-2-2? Fewer switches (6 in 12 points, not 11), and nobody is always chasing. It matches the Thue–Morse sequence for four symbols.

Football tried ABBA for shootouts in 2017 and dropped it in 2018. In 2,621 pro tiebreaks, serving first made no significant difference.
