Why does tennis look faster live than on TV?

Take the same 200 km/h serve. Courtside, it sweeps about 138° across your view every second; on TV, only about 19°. That's roughly 7× faster.

TV's main camera sits high behind the baseline, so the 23.77 m court is squashed in the lens and the ball flies almost straight toward the camera and back. Our eyes are less sensitive to motion in depth. Courtside, the ball sweeps sideways, past the eye's ~70°/s smooth-pursuit limit, so it seems to "vanish" for a moment. That's what "fast" feels like.

Football is the other way round: from the stands you see a whole 105 m pitch, while TV frames only about 40 m. In 1931, psychologist J.F. Brown found that the same speed looks slower in a bigger window.

Angular speeds and times are simplified-model estimates; camera position and seats are illustrative assumptions.
