No. 7 on the official list. So why is Djokovic called the king of the return?

2011 US Open SF, 3-5 in the fifth, Federer holding two match points: Djokovic hits a forehand return winner and wins four straight games. Yet the ATP's all-time Return Rating has him No. 7.

A 200 km/h serve reaches the returner in about 0.6–0.7 s. A simple human reaction takes about 0.2 s. Elite returners take off as the server strikes, land 0.15 s later and start the racket at 0.19 s — and still have to pick a side. They guess before they see the ball clearly.

The clues: the ball toss, the arm and racket, the upper body. A split step makes the start 12–13% faster.

The numbers: No. 7 all-time, No. 2 on hard courts, No. 1 among active players; 38.8% of return games won in 2011; about 80% of returns past the service line, the deepest among the greats; 12-0 vs Raonic, 10-2 vs Isner.
