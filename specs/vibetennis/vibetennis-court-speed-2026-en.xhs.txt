Why is clay slow and grass fast?

Same ball, same speed — clay, grass and hard courts send back three different balls.

The secret is in the milliseconds of contact: the ball's bottom slides, then the court grips it. Friction decides how much speed it keeps; bounce decides how high it kicks.

Clay friction reaches 0.9 at low angles, and the ball digs a dent and kicks up. Wimbledon grass: 0.5–0.6, low and fast.

Hard courts vary with the sand in the top coat: big angular grit slows the ball, fine round grit speeds it up.
