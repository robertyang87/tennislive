Why does a tennis ball wear fuzz? It's a brake — and a stabiliser

Shave a tennis ball bald and the same 200 km/h serve could arrive at about 130 or about 180 km/h after one court length (drag-law estimate, no gravity or bounce). A fuzzy ball arrives at about 120, every time.

In 1912 Eiffel found that past a certain speed a smooth ball's drag suddenly drops from about 0.5 to about 0.1: the drag crisis.

Golf balls use dimples to trigger it early. Footballs sit on the cliff edge and knuckle. Pro tennis speeds, 100–250 km/h, sit right in a smooth ball's cliff zone — and the felt flattens the cliff: drag coefficient about 0.6, with no cliff, in three independent measurements.

Fluffier felt means more drag, which is why players pick a tight ball to serve. The ITF's type 3 ball is about 6% bigger, same weight, to slow play on fast courts.
